# Einstein's Method

> If you cannot explain a concept simply enough that it seems obvious,
> you do not understand it well enough to enshrine it. Radical
> simplification is the most honest test of conceptual maturity.

## Origin

Attributed to Albert Einstein: "If you can't explain it simply, you
don't understand it well enough." Whether or not the quote is
authentic, the principle stands — the mark of a well-designed concept
is that it feels inevitable once understood.

## Application to Problem Analysis

When evaluating long-term soundness, Einstein's Method tests whether a
concept or solution is simple enough to survive years of use and
evolution.

### 1. Explain in One Sentence

Write exactly one sentence:

> "This concept lets the [stakeholder] [do X]."

If the sentence contains "and", "but", "except", "however", or
"unless", the concept is not simple enough. Break it down.

### 2. Explain to a Non-Expert

Could someone familiar with the domain understand the concept from the
one-sentence explanation alone? If they must ask "What does that
mean?", the concept still carries accidental complexity.

### 3. Remove One Thing

What is the minimal viable subset that still delivers the core benefit?

- If you remove the most complex rule, does it still work?
- If you remove the most common use case, is it still worth having?
- If the subset is empty, the full proposal may be too complex for its
  own good.

### 4. Check for Obviousness

After the explanation, does the listener's reaction feel like "of
course, that's the natural way"? If the reaction is "interesting, I
never thought of it that way" or "clever", the concept still has
novelty masquerading as insight. A well-designed concept should feel
like it was always missing.

## When to Use

This method suits complexity, clarity, and longevity concerns: "too
complex", "doesn't age well", "hard to explain".

## Output

The one-sentence explanation (or the finding that it cannot be
written), the non-expert test result, the minimal viable subset, and
the obviousness verdict.
