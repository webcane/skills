# Pipelines: Sequencing Multiple Frameworks

A pipeline is justified only when the phases do different jobs: prepare,
open, navigate, close. If one framework covers all four, use one. Never run
more than three frameworks in a single answer.

For each phase, the router should name the framework, the concrete move, and
the exit condition — the signal that it is time to move to the next phase.

---

## Pipeline 1: A difficult conversation

**Sequence:** Difficult Conversations → NVC → Active Listening

| Phase | Framework | Move | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prepare | Difficult Conversations | Map the Three Conversations: What Happened, Feelings, Identity. Separate contribution from blame. | You can state your contribution and their likely perspective without a script |
| Open | Difficult Conversations | Use the Third Story: describe the gap between your view and theirs, not their fault | They engage with the gap instead of defending |
| Express | NVC | Observation, feeling, need, request — one incident, no history dump | They understand what you are asking for |
| Receive | Active Listening | Paraphrase and reflect before answering | They relax, correct you, or say "yes, exactly" |
| Close | Difficult Conversations + Getting to Yes | Agree on what changes and who does what by when | A concrete, checkable commitment |

Use when: the topic has been avoided, identity is at stake, or the same fight
keeps recurring. Do not use when there is abuse, coercion, or a threat of
violence — switch to Safety.

## Pipeline 2: Negotiating a deal

**Sequence:** Getting to Yes → Never Split the Difference → Influence

| Phase | Framework | Move | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prepare | Getting to Yes | Clarify interests behind positions, your BATNA, their likely BATNA, and objective criteria | You know your walk-away point and three possible trades |
| Open | Getting to Yes | Ask what matters to them beyond the number; separate people from the problem | You hear an interest, not only a position |
| Navigate | Never Split | Tactical empathy: labels, mirrors, calibrated questions, accusation audit | They say "that's right" or reveal a constraint |
| Close | Influence + Getting to Yes | Use commitment, reciprocity, or social proof only when true; confirm the deal against objective criteria | Both sides can state the agreement in one sentence |

Use when: money, salary, contract terms, deadlines, or resources are at stake
and the counterpart has authority to say yes. Do not use when the counterpart
is acting in bad faith or has no incentive to deal — protect your BATNA and
consider walking away.

Russian-context note: the first number is often a test, not a position. Do not
counter immediately; ask what is behind it.

## Pipeline 3: Giving feedback

**Sequence:** SCARF → Radical Candor → Active Listening

| Phase | Framework | Move | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prepare | SCARF | Which domain is threatened: status, certainty, autonomy, relatedness, fairness? Choose a private setting and a specific incident | You can name the threat and remove one unnecessary one |
| Deliver | Radical Candor | Care personally, challenge directly. Use SBI: Situation, Behavior, Impact. One incident, no character judgments | They understand what happened and why it matters |
| Receive | Active Listening | Ask for their view; reflect before defending or correcting | They have said their part without being cut off |
| Close | Radical Candor + Getting to Yes | Agree on one specific change and a follow-up date | A checkable commitment exists |

Use when: the user manages or works closely with the person and the behavior
can change. Do not use for public correction, for a person in acute distress,
or as a substitute for a formal performance process.

## Pipeline 4: Persuading a group

**Sequence:** Influence → Storytelling → Socratic

| Phase | Framework | Move | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design | Influence | Choose the principles that are true here: reciprocity, commitment, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity. Drop the ones that would be manipulative | The argument rests on two or three real levers, not all seven |
| Deliver | Storytelling | Wrap the message in a concrete narrative: a person, a problem, a turn, a result. Use STAR for a short update, PAS for a pitch, Sparkline for a longer talk | The audience can retell the point in one sentence |
| Engage | Socratic | Ask questions that let the audience reach the conclusion themselves | Someone in the audience states the conclusion as their own |

Use when: the user needs buy-in from a group, not compliance from one person.
Do not use when the audience is hostile and safety is missing; restore safety
with Crucial Conversations first.

## Pipeline 5: Resolving team conflict

**Sequence:** Crucial Conversations → NVC → Getting to Yes

| Phase | Framework | Move | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open | Crucial Conversations | Establish mutual purpose and safety: "I want a solution that works for both of you." Separate facts from stories | Both parties agree the conversation is worth having |
| Express | NVC | Each party states observation, feeling, need. No diagnosis of the other side's motives | Each person can state the other's need without caricature |
| Resolve | Getting to Yes | Move from positions to interests; generate options; apply objective criteria | A workable option is on the table |
| Close | Crucial Conversations | Agree who does what by when, and how the team will check | The agreement is written down or repeated back |

Use when: two or more people are in a recurring conflict and the user is a
participant or a mediator. If the user is the mediator, get permission from
both sides before facilitating.

## Pipeline 6: Motivating someone resistant to change

**Sequence:** SCARF → Motivational Interviewing → Socratic

| Phase | Framework | Move | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Understand | SCARF | Which threat explains the resistance: autonomy, certainty, status, relatedness, fairness? | You can name the threat without blaming the person |
| Explore | Motivational Interviewing | Do not push. Ask open questions, affirm, reflect, summarize. Elicit their own reasons for change | You hear change talk, not only sustain talk |
| Deepen | Socratic | Ask questions that let them see the implications of their own reasoning | They state a reason and a next step in their own words |

Use when: the person is ambivalent, not defiant, and the user has time. Do not
use when a deadline or a safety rule requires a direct instruction; state the
requirement clearly and then use MI for the reaction.

---

## Pipeline 7: Saying no without burning the bridge

**Sequence:** Active Listening → DESC → NVC

| Phase | Framework | Move | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hear | Active Listening | Understand the request and the need behind it before answering | The person feels heard and stops repeating the request |
| Assert | DESC | Describe the facts, express the impact, specify the boundary, state the positive consequence | The boundary is clear and does not sound like a threat |
| Connect | NVC | Acknowledge the unmet need and offer a real alternative if one exists | The relationship survives the no |

Use when: the user must refuse a client, a boss, a colleague, or a family
member and wants to preserve the relationship. Do not use when the request is
abusive or repeated after a clear no — then the boundary is the whole message,
and consequences may be necessary.

Russian-context note: a soft opening followed by a concrete alternative lands
better than a bare «нет». Example: «Я не смогу взять это на этой неделе. Могу
в следующий вторник или подскажу, к кому обратиться».

---

## Pipeline quality checks

- Does each phase have a different job? If two phases do the same job, cut one.
- Is there a listening phase before a speaking phase? If not, justify why.
- Is the close concrete? "We agreed to communicate better" is not a close.
- Is the pipeline reversible? If the other person escalates, can the user fall
  back to safety or listening without losing the goal?
