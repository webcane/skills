# Quick Reference: Thirteen Frameworks on One Page

Use this file for `select` mode, for a knowledge question, or as a fast
reminder before opening a framework file.

## At a glance

| Framework | Core idea | Best for | One diagnostic question |
|---|---|---|---|
| NVC | Observation → feeling → need → request | Expressing without blame | "What did a camera see, and what need of mine was touched?" |
| Radical Candor | Care personally + challenge directly | Feedback and management | "Do they know I care, and do they know exactly what to change?" |
| Crucial Conversations | Make it safe to talk about anything | High stakes, strong emotion | "Is there mutual purpose and mutual respect in the room?" |
| Difficult Conversations | Three conversations: What Happened, Feelings, Identity | Avoided or recurring talks | "What identity is threatened — mine or theirs?" |
| Never Split the Difference | Tactical empathy + calibrated questions | Negotiation under pressure | "What is their constraint, and how do I make it safe to say no?" |
| Getting to Yes | Interests, not positions; BATNA | Principled negotiation | "What do they actually need, and what is my walk-away?" |
| Influence | Seven principles of ethical persuasion | Persuasion and defense | "Which lever is true here, and would I be comfortable naming it?" |
| Storytelling | Structure that makes people feel and remember | Pitches, talks, leadership | "Who is the hero, what changes, and why should they care?" |
| Motivational Interviewing | Evoke their own reasons for change | Ambivalence and resistance | "Am I arguing for change more than they are?" |
| Socratic Method | Questions that lead to insight | Teaching, coaching, inquiry | "Is this question for their thinking or for my victory?" |
| SCARF | Status, Certainty, Autonomy, Relatedness, Fairness | Diagnosing threat and resistance | "Which of the five is under threat right now?" |
| Active Listening | Empathic presence and reflection | Any emotional conversation | "Can I repeat their point so well that they say 'exactly'?" |
| DESC | Describe, Express, Specify, Consequences | Boundaries and assertiveness | "What exactly am I asking for, and what happens if we agree?" |

## NVC — Nonviolent Communication

- **Core**: separate observation from evaluation, feeling from thought, need
  from strategy, request from demand.
- **Key moves**: "When I saw/heard X, I felt Y, because I need Z. Would you be
  willing to W?"
- **Watch out**: mechanical delivery; using the formula to manipulate; skipping
  self-empathy when flooded.

## Radical Candor

- **Core**: care personally and challenge directly at the same time.
- **Quadrants**: Ruinous Empathy (care, no challenge), Obnoxious Aggression
  (challenge, no care), Manipulative Insincerity (neither), Radical Candor
  (both).
- **Key moves**: SBI — Situation, Behavior, Impact; immediate, specific, private.
- **Watch out**: feedback sandwich; saving it for the annual review; "just being
  honest" as an excuse for cruelty.

## Crucial Conversations

- **Core**: people stop talking when safety is missing. Restore safety, then
  return to the content.
- **Key moves**: start with heart, learn to look, make it safe, master my
  stories, STATE my path, explore others' paths, move to action.
- **Watch out**: winning the argument and losing the relationship; silence or
  violence disguised as "being professional".

## Difficult Conversations

- **Core**: every hard conversation is three conversations — What Happened,
  Feelings, Identity.
- **Key moves**: move from truth to contribution, acknowledge feelings before
  solving, ground identity so a mistake does not become a verdict on the self,
  open with the Third Story.
- **Watch out**: leading with the conclusion; assuming intent from impact;
  using it with an abusive or much more powerful counterpart.

## Never Split the Difference

- **Core**: tactical empathy plus calibrated questions. "No" is often the start
  of a real conversation.
- **Key moves**: mirroring, labeling, accusation audit, calibrated "how"
  questions, "that's right", Ackerman bargaining, black swans.
- **Watch out**: tactics without genuine curiosity feel like a script; not for
  relationships where trust is the point.

## Getting to Yes

- **Core**: separate people from the problem; focus on interests, not positions;
  invent options for mutual gain; insist on objective criteria.
- **Key moves**: BATNA analysis, interest mapping, option generation, one-text
  procedure, negotiation jujitsu.
- **Watch out**: assumes a good-faith counterpart and enough time; not a
  substitute for leverage when the other side is coercive.

## Influence

- **Core**: seven principles — reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social
  proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity.
- **Key moves**: pick the two or three that are true; make the influence
  transparent; teach the user to detect manipulation.
- **Watch out**: using scarcity or authority as a trick; persuasion without
  consent becomes manipulation.

## Storytelling

- **Core**: a concrete person, a problem, a turn, a result. Facts tell, stories
  move.
- **Frameworks**: Hero's Journey (12 or 5 stages), STAR, PAS/PASO, Pixar Story
  Spine, Duarte's Sparkline, conclusion-first (BLUF).
- **Watch out**: data dump with "once upon a time"; the hero is the audience,
  not the speaker; burying the lead.

## Motivational Interviewing

- **Core**: partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation. Roll with
  resistance instead of pushing against it.
- **Key moves**: OARS — open questions, affirmations, reflections, summaries;
  DARN-CAT change talk; readiness ruler.
- **Watch out**: the righting reflex; premature planning; arguing for change
  harder than the person does.

## Socratic Method

- **Core**: six question types — clarification, assumptions, evidence,
  perspectives, implications, meta-questions.
- **Key moves**: ask one question at a time, leave silence, follow the answer
  rather than a script.
- **Watch out**: the gotcha trap; question bombing; using questions as a power
  play against a junior person.

## SCARF

- **Core**: five social domains drive threat or reward — Status, Certainty,
  Autonomy, Relatedness, Fairness.
- **Key moves**: name the threatened domain, remove one threat before adding a
  request, design the setting.
- **Watch out**: it is a diagnostic lens, not a script; do not announce
  "your SCARF is threatened" to the other person.

## Active Listening

- **Core**: listen to understand, not to reply. Reflection is the proof.
- **Key moves**: paraphrase content, reflect feeling, summarize, clarify, use
  silence, notice non-verbal signals.
- **Watch out**: roadblocks — advising, consoling, one-upping, educating,
  correcting; reflection as a technique for steering.

## DESC

- **Core**: Describe facts, Express impact, Specify the request, state the
  positive Consequences.
- **Key moves**: camera-testable facts, one incident, one feeling, one specific
  request, a benefit rather than a threat.
- **Watch out**: stacking grievances; skipping the feeling; vague requests;
  leading with "or else".

## Choosing in ten seconds

- Need to understand → Active Listening.
- Need to express a need → NVC or DESC.
- Need to persuade → Influence + Storytelling.
- Need an agreement → Getting to Yes + Never Split.
- Need to give feedback → SCARF + Radical Candor.
- Need to resolve a conflict → Crucial Conversations + NVC.
- Need a hard talk → Difficult Conversations + Active Listening.
- Need to motivate change → Motivational Interviewing + Socratic.
- Need a boundary → DESC + Radical Candor.
- Need rapport → Active Listening + SCARF.
- Need to teach → Socratic + Storytelling.
- Not sure → observe, listen, empathize, express, collaborate, commit.
