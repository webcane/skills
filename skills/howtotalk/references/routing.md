# Routing: Choosing the Right Framework

The router in `SKILL.md` gives the short version. This file is the expanded
map: how to read the situation, how emotion and power change the choice, and
when the obvious framework is the wrong one.

## The four questions that decide the route

1. **What is the job?** Understand, express, persuade, negotiate, give
   feedback, resolve conflict, set a boundary, motivate change, teach, or
   apologize. A conversation can contain several jobs; pick the dominant one
   for the opening.
2. **What is the emotional temperature?** Low, medium, high, or crisis. Above
   medium, analytical frameworks wait; listening and safety come first.
3. **What is the power dynamic?** The user is up, down, lateral, or the
   relationship is asymmetrical in a way that makes direct speech costly.
4. **What is at stake if it goes wrong?** A task, a relationship, a reputation,
   money, safety, or a core identity. High stakes raise the cost of directness
   and the value of preparation.

If the answers point in different directions, safety wins, then the
relationship, then the task.

## Decision flow

```
START
  |
  +-- Immediate danger or abuse? --> Safety protocol, not a framework
  |
  +-- Primary goal?
        Understand someone ....... Active Listening (+ SCARF)
        Express a need .......... NVC or DESC
        Persuade ................ Influence + Storytelling
        Negotiate ............... Getting to Yes + Never Split
        Give feedback ........... SCARF + Radical Candor
        Resolve conflict ........ Crucial Conversations + NVC
        Have a hard talk ........ Difficult Conversations + Active Listening
        Motivate change ......... Motivational Interviewing + Socratic
        Set a boundary .......... DESC + Radical Candor
        Build rapport ........... Active Listening + SCARF
        Teach or coach .......... Socratic + Storytelling
        Apologize ............... Difficult Conversations + NVC
        Not sure ................ Universal fallback
```

## Emotion levels

### Crisis (10/10)

Only safety and Active Listening are available. The prefrontal cortex is
offline; logic, options, and scripts will not land. Goal: reduce the
temperature and connect the person to help. Do not run a negotiation or a
feedback conversation in this state.

### High (7–9/10)

Use Active Listening, SCARF, and NVC observation plus feeling only. No
requests, no solutions, no "let's be rational". Goal: validation, reflection,
safety. When the person exhales, ask permission to move to the practical part.

### Medium (4–6/10)

All frameworks are available. This is the productive zone. Choose by job, not
by temperature.

### Low (1–3/10)

Analytical frameworks work best: DESC, Getting to Yes, Radical Candor. Emotional
reflection can feel forced; use it sparingly and name the feeling only when it
is visible.

## Power dynamics

| Position | What changes | Practical adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| User is up (boss, client, parent) | Directness is cheap for the user and expensive for the other person | Ask more, assert less; make it safe to disagree; avoid public correction |
| User is down (report, junior, dependent) | Directness can cost the user their job or safety | Prepare evidence; use questions and objective criteria; choose the setting; consider a witness or a paper trail |
| Lateral (peer, partner, sibling) | No formal authority; the relationship is the leverage | Use mutual purpose, reciprocity, and explicit agreements |
| Asymmetrical by dependency | The other person cannot freely say no | Treat "yes" with suspicion; offer real options; do not mistake compliance for agreement |

In high power-distance settings, a public challenge reads as a status attack
even when the content is right. Move the conversation private, keep the
opening soft, and put the substance in questions or objective criteria.

## Cultural context

These are tendencies, not rules about individuals.

- **High-context** (Russia, Japan, China, much of the Arab world): meaning is
  carried by tone, relationship, timing, and what is not said. Open indirectly,
  protect face, and avoid putting someone on the spot in front of others.
- **Low-context** (US, Germany, Netherlands, Scandinavia): explicit requests and
  direct disagreement are normal and often expected. Excessive indirectness
  reads as evasive.
- **High power-distance** (Russia, Japan, many hierarchical organizations):
  status and relatedness are amplified. Do not challenge a superior publicly;
  use a private channel or an intermediary.
- **Uncertainty-avoiding** (Germany, Japan, Russia in many organizations):
  certainty is amplified. Give timelines, criteria, and a clear next step.
- **Egalitarian** (Scandinavia, Netherlands): fairness and autonomy are
  amplified. Explain the why and leave room for choice.

Russia-specific notes for scripts:

- «Вы» is the default at work and with strangers; switching to «ты» is a
  relationship move, not a formatting choice.
- Имя and отчество signal respect in formal or older settings; follow the
  user's lead.
- Public criticism, even mild, can be heard as humiliation. Praise in public,
  correct in private.
- A direct "нет" can feel abrupt. A soft opening plus a concrete alternative
  preserves the relationship without diluting the boundary.
- Emotional intensity is not automatically aggression. Read the content, not
  only the volume.

## When the obvious framework is wrong

| Situation | Obvious choice | Better choice | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feedback to a defensive senior person | Radical Candor | SCARF + private Socratic questions | Direct challenge threatens status and invites retaliation |
| Negotiation with no good faith | Getting to Yes | Never Split + BATNA + walk-away plan | Principled negotiation assumes a counterpart who wants a deal |
| Conflict with an abusive partner | NVC | Safety planning | "Finding your contribution" can shift blame to the victim |
| Someone in acute distress | Difficult Conversations | Active Listening only | Analysis escalates the flooding |
| Persuading a hostile audience | Storytelling | Never Split + Crucial Conversations | A story cannot restore safety that was never there |
| Saying no to a client | DESC | Active Listening → DESC → Getting to Yes | The client needs to feel heard before the boundary lands |
| Teaching a resistant adult | Socratic | Motivational Interviewing | Questions feel like entrapment when the person has not chosen to change |
| Apologizing to a subordinate | NVC | Difficult Conversations + concrete repair | The power gap makes vague empathy feel like spin |

## Framework compatibility

### Pairs that reinforce each other

- **NVC + Active Listening**: listen empathically, then express without blame.
- **SCARF + Radical Candor**: diagnose the threat, then deliver the feedback.
- **Getting to Yes + Never Split**: structure plus tactical execution.
- **Crucial Conversations + Difficult Conversations**: safety plus depth.
- **Motivational Interviewing + Socratic**: questions that evoke rather than
  install.

### Pairs that pull in different directions

- **Radical Candor + NVC**: candor pushes directness; NVC pushes gentle
  expression. Resolve by being direct about the observation and the impact,
  and gentle about the request.
- **Never Split + Getting to Yes**: Voss distrusts joint problem-solving;
  Fisher and Ury build on it. Use Getting to Yes for structure and Never Split
  for the emotional tactics inside it.
- **DESC + Active Listening**: speaking versus receiving. Sequence them:
  listen first, then DESC.
- **Influence + NVC**: influence can feel manipulative; NVC is about
  authenticity. Use influence principles transparently and only when they are
  true.

## Route quality checks

Before answering, check:

- Is the primary framework doing the main job, or is it just the most famous
  one?
- Does the plan have a phase where the other person gets to speak?
- Is there a script for the most likely pushback, not only for the opening?
- Would a reasonable person in this culture and power position actually say
  these words?
- If the user follows the advice and it goes wrong, is there a safe next step?
