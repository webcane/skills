# Dialectical Method — Thesis · Antithesis · Synthesis

> Pit a claim against its opposite and let the contradiction produce a
> more complete truth. The synthesis is not a compromise — it is a
> higher claim that absorbs what was valid on both sides.

## Origin

Formalized by Hegel (thesis — antithesis — synthesis triad) and
inherited by Marx; the pattern of reasoning by contradiction is older
and appears wherever thinking advances by opposing views.

## Application to Problem Analysis

When a hypothesis is asserted with confidence, the dialectical method
forces it to meet its opposite and checks whether a sharper statement
survives.

### 1. State the Thesis

Write the claim as strongly as it is held.

> "Remote work reduces productivity."

### 2. Construct the Antithesis

Build the strongest opposing claim — not a straw man.

> "Remote work raises productivity through flexibility."

### 3. Let Them Conflict

Find what is valid in each side and what each side ignores. The
conflict must be real; if the two claims do not actually clash, the
thesis was under-specified.

### 4. Produce the Synthesis

Derive the claim that accounts for both.

> "Productivity under remote work depends on the type of task and the
> employee's level of self-organization."

This synthesis is a new, more precise hypothesis than either pole.

## When to Use

An overconfident claim, a polarized debate ("it's either X or Y"), or a
hypothesis that has never been forced to face its opposite.

## Output

The thesis, the honest antithesis, the conflict analysis, and the
synthesis (a more precise hypothesis) — or a finding that the original
claim was under-specified.
