# ICE Scoring — Prioritizing Hypotheses

> Score each hypothesis on three 1–10 criteria — Impact, Confidence,
> Ease — and multiply: Impact × Confidence × Ease. The highest score
> gets tested first. ICE turns a pile of hypotheses into an explicit
> ranking instead of an argument.

## Origin

A prioritization method common in growth and product practice (Sean
Ellis's ICE framework, popularized with growth hacking). It is scoring,
not science — the numbers make the ranking visible and debatable.

## Application to Problem Analysis

When many hypotheses have been generated, ICE orders them for testing.

### 1. Impact (1–10)

How strongly will this hypothesis affect the key metric if it holds?

### 2. Confidence (1–10)

How confident are you in the data and assumptions behind it? (Low for
untested assumptions.)

### 3. Ease (1–10)

How easy and cheap is it to test? (High for a cheap test.)

### 4. Compute and Rank

Score = Impact × Confidence × Ease. Rank hypotheses by score and state
why the top one leads.

## When to Use

More than one candidate hypothesis, limited testing capacity, or a
debate about which hypothesis to try first.

## Output

A scored table of hypotheses (Impact, Confidence, Ease, product), the
ranked order, and the rationale for the top pick.
