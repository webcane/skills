# RAT — Riskiest Assumption Test

> Find the assumption in the hypothesis whose failure would sink the
> whole project, and test that one first — by the fastest, cheapest
> means available. If the riskiest assumption holds, confidence in the
> hypothesis rises; if it fails, the project pivots before it gets
> expensive.

## Origin

Part of lean-startup practice (Eric Ries; the "riskiest assumption
test" framing) — validate the assumption with the most downside before
building on top of it.

## Application to Problem Analysis

After a hypothesis is formed, RAT identifies the single assumption that
matters most and designs a minimal test for it.

### 1. List the Assumptions

Break the hypothesis into the assumptions it silently makes.

### 2. Rank by Risk

The riskiest assumption is the one whose failure invalidates the whole
hypothesis — not the one that is merely uncertain.

### 3. Design the Cheapest Test

Find the fastest, cheapest way to test just that assumption. Do not
build the full solution.

> For "we will launch a paid subscription because users are willing to
> pay", the riskiest assumption is "willing to pay". Test it not by
> building the product but with a landing page and a "Buy" button, and
> measure conversion.

### 4. Act on the Result

If the test fails, the hypothesis is dead or must change — cheaply. If
it passes, proceed to the next riskiest assumption.

## When to Use

A hypothesis that makes a big bet on one unverified assumption, a
project whose failure cost is high, or a need to learn the most
important thing with the least investment.

## Output

The assumptions listed, the riskiest one identified and why, the
cheapest test designed, and the decision rule for acting on the result.
