# Socratic Method

> Disciplined questioning to expose hidden contradictions, imprecise
> definitions, and unstated assumptions. A problem or proposal that
> survives Socratic interrogation without introducing special cases is
> internally sound.

## Origin

Attributed to Socrates as recorded in Plato's dialogues. The method
proceeds by asking a series of questions to expose the limits of a
definition or argument. It does not assume the answer — it tests the
reasoning.

## Application to Problem Analysis

When analyzing any problem, proposal, or decision, the Socratic Method
forces every term and rule to be precisely defined and tests whether
they hold under scrutiny.

### 1. Define All Terms

For every concept the problem introduces, ask:

- "What exactly does *this* mean?"
- "Is this term defined in the project's established terminology?"
- "Does this definition conflict with any existing definition?"

### 2. Test with Counterexamples

Take the problem's rules and apply them to edge cases:

- "If this holds, what happens when we apply it to [edge case]?"
- "Is the outcome consistent with how other constructs behave in the
  same situation?"

### 3. Follow the Contradiction

When two criteria seem to conflict, do not resolve by intuition:

- "Which principle gives way?"
- "Is the exception principled (derived from a higher rule) or
  arbitrary (introduced just to patch this case)?"
- "Would the same reasoning apply if roles were reversed?"

### 4. Play Devil's Advocate

Construct the strongest argument against the proposal using only its
own definitions. If the counterargument reveals a flaw, the proposal
must be revised — not dismissed.

## When to Use

This method suits problems exhibiting ambiguous terms, unstated
assumptions, or possible internal contradictions. It is the lowest-cost
probe and often the first method to try when nothing else is obvious.

## Output

A set of precisely defined terms, a list of counterexamples tested, any
contradictions found (with whether they are principled or arbitrary),
and the devil's-advocate verdict on whether the proposal survives.
