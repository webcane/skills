# Six Thinking Hats — Structured Multi-Perspective Review

> Wear one colored hat at a time. Each hat permits exactly one kind of
> thinking — facts, emotion, criticism, optimism, creativity, or process
> control. Switching hats keeps emotion from leaking into logic and
> makes every perspective explicit.

## Origin

Developed by Edward de Bono in 1985 (*Six Thinking Hats*) as a way to
structure group and individual thinking so that all modes of thought
are given their turn.

## Application to Problem Analysis

When a hypothesis or proposal needs a full review, run it through all
six hats — one at a time — and record what each hat sees.

### The Six Hats

- **White (facts)** — What data do we have? What is missing?
- **Red (emotion)** — How do I feel about this? (No justification
  required.)
- **Black (criticism)** — What are the risks, weaknesses, and failure
  modes?
- **Yellow (optimism)** — What are the benefits and opportunities?
- **Green (creativity)** — What alternatives exist? What if we changed
  the approach?
- **Blue (process)** — What have we covered and what should we do
  next?

### For Hypotheses

Apply all six hats to a single hypothesis. The Black hat may reveal
that "the new design will raise sales" ignores technical risk, while
the Green hat produces "what if we make the design adaptive instead of
new?"

## When to Use

A proposal that needs to be examined from every side, a decision being
argued from one dominant perspective, or a group discussion where
emotion and logic are getting mixed.

## Output

One set of findings per hat, a consolidated view of the proposal's
strengths, risks, and alternatives, and a process note on what to do
next.
